# Exact exchanges and color-preserving fallback chains

The October 2026 precompute checks all 251,486 unordered **model-address pairs**
across the 34 current puzzle models. None admits the literal permutation that
exchanges only those two addresses and fixes every other address. These are
complete group-membership results, not timeouts. Some geometric models include
empty pose addresses, so this count is not a count of physical sticker pairs.

This does **not** prohibit exchanging the two selected stickers while restoring
all other **colors**. Two other stickers of the same color can exchange without
changing their displayed colors. This release adds 2,760 verified local fallback
chains across 16 models, requiring 227,360 extra bytes for their operation records.
The largest new stored chain has 32 primitive moves. Models without a useful new
chain retain the existing search methods. No chain is claimed globally shortest.

## Exact membership and pair-orbit compression

Let `d` be the number of model addresses, `m` the number of primitive moves, and
`G` the subgroup of the permutation group on those addresses generated by those
moves. Source-to-destination arrays represent permutations in `O(d)` storage.
Let `t(a,b)` denote the exact transposition of addresses `a` and `b`.

For any legal permutation `g`, conjugation maps a transposition to another
transposition:

\[
g\,t(a,b)\,g^{-1}=t(g(a),g(b)).
\]

Consequently membership in `G` is constant on each generator orbit of unordered
pairs. Breadth-first traversal of the **pair action** has at most `d(d-1)/2`
vertices and `m` transitions per vertex. These are pairs of addresses, not whole
puzzle configurations. Test only one representative per pair orbit against the
existing complete stabilizer chain. Total pair-orbit work is `O(m d²)` and the
temporary pair-visited array uses `O(d²)` bytes.

The complete chain fixes successive base addresses. A target permutation is
sifted by right-composing inverse transversal representatives until it becomes
the identity. A missing transversal entry proves nonmembership. A full successful
sift gives a legal word by reversing the order of the selected representatives.
At most `d` levels each compose `d` entries, so one sift costs `O(d²)`, with the
already stored chain as input. Representative words remain DAG-compressed until
a requested word is expanded. Expansion and playback length are separately
bounded and never confused with membership.

`tools/precompute-transpositions.cjs` recomputes the audit and adds sparse words.
The per-model result is stored in each color cache's `exactTranspositions` field;
the aggregate report is `data/color-solver/transposition-report.json`.

## Reusable sparse fallback library

The precompute inspects complete-chain transversal representatives for exact
double transpositions `t(a,b)t(c,d)`. It expands bounded words, adds one-generator
conjugates to reposition them, and deduplicates by the full sparse permutation.
It keeps at most 512 new words and 512 KiB of operation records per puzzle.
Every retained word is independently replayed through the original generators;
the sparse record must match every moved and fixed address exactly.

The existing eligibility and manual-solver catalog paths already consume these
words. A sparse word becomes an available result only after checking the **current**
held colors and independently replaying the word. A cache miss remains unknown.
The precompute is exhaustive for single-transposition membership, but deliberately
bounded and incomplete for double-transposition witnesses.

## Additional on-demand compensation scan

When a manual color-held request has no catalog witness, a bounded scan uses the
complete chain to test:

\[
t(a,b),\qquad t(a,b)t(c,d)\quad\text{where }\{c,d\}\cap\{a,b\}=\varnothing,
\quad \mathrm{color}(c)=\mathrm{color}(d).
\]

Only currently occupied addresses are considered for `c,d`. Every accepted
permutation fixes all identities except the selected pair and at most one
equal-colored pair. Thus it restores **every other color**, including held colors.
The exact selected identities still exchange; matching only their colors would
not suffice.

The scan keeps its pair cursor across manual search continuations. One invocation
receives at most 250 ms from the user's existing cumulative search budget. The
candidate family has `O(d²)` pairs and a direct worst-case `O(d⁴)` arithmetic cost
if every candidate requires a full `O(d²)` sift. Typical nonmembers fail early.
Memory is bounded independently, including lazy inverse-transversal tables and
word-expansion workspace. Small memory budgets skip this optional fallback.

A successful factorization is expanded subject to the existing move limit,
verified against the original generators and current request, and then replayed
by the game. Exhausting this family means only that **one compensating pair did
not provide a witness**. Longer cycles, several compensators, or different color
arrangements may still work. Existing broader search therefore continues; a
bounded miss or long word never becomes an impossibility claim.

For example, a tested solved 4×4×4 request exchanges model addresses 10 and 22
while preserving all 94 other colors. A cached 10-move chain also exchanges the
equal-colored stickers at 70 and 73. The on-demand scan independently finds a
verified witness when the macro catalog and projected BFS are disabled.

The color condition depends on the current state. A single state-independent
table with one guaranteed color-preserving chain for every pair cannot replace
all manual searches; some requests are physically unreachable and some require
more general compensating permutations than this bounded family.
